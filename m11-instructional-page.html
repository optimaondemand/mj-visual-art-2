📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What if you painted a place the way it felt, not the way it looked?
Some artists try to copy the world exactly. Vincent van Gogh did something different. He used bold color, swirling lines, and thick, energetic brushwork to put feeling and movement into a landscape. Today you look closely at how he did it, so that next week you can paint a place the way it feels to you.

🎨 LOOK FIRST
Vincent van Gogh, The Starry Night, 1889 A.D. Public domain.

Before you read another word, just look. The sky is not still. Van Gogh painted it with long, curling strokes that spin like wind. The stars glow like they are alive. Hold that feeling. The rest of this lesson explains the choices behind it.

📚 PART 1 · WHAT IS POST-IMPRESSIONISM?

Who was Vincent van Gogh?

Vincent van Gogh was a Dutch artist born in 1853 A.D. He became one of the most famous painters in history, but he sold very few paintings while he was alive. In about ten years he made more than 2,000 artworks, including many landscapes that show nature full of movement and emotion. He died in 1890 A.D.

A change in goals

When Van Gogh started, many artists were working in Impressionism. Impressionist painters used light colors and loose brushstrokes to capture a quick moment, like sunlight on water. Over time, some artists felt that style did not show strong feelings. That led to Post-Impressionism, a style built on personal expression, bold color, and dramatic brushwork. Instead of painting nature exactly as it looked, Van Gogh painted it the way it felt to him.

💡 ONE WORD TO KNOW
Impasto is paint laid on thick, so you can see the ridges and even the marks of the brush. It makes a surface look full of energy and gives the artwork real texture you could almost feel.
👁 PART 2 · LOOK CLOSELY: MOVEMENT AND EMOTION

Van Gogh, Wheatfield with Crows

Vincent van Gogh, Wheatfield with Crows, 1890 A.D. Public domain.

A wide golden field sits under a heavy dark-blue sky. Black crows scatter upward, and a red-brown path cuts through the center. The whole painting feels tense and dramatic, and Van Gogh built that feeling with the Elements of Art.

🔍 FIND THE ELEMENTS
Color in the clash of golden wheat against the stormy blue sky. Line in the short, choppy strokes and the crows. Movement, because your eye sweeps up the path and out with the birds. Emphasis on the dark crows that pull your eye first. The thick, broken brushwork gives the field its rough texture.
📚 PART 3 · WATCH THE LINE WORK

Van Gogh, The Olive Trees

Vincent van Gogh, The Olive Trees, 1889 A.D. Public domain.

Van Gogh loved painting outdoors in southern France. Here the olive trees twist and curl, the hills roll in waves, and even the cloud seems to swirl. He used twisting lines and thick layers of paint, called impasto, to make trees, fields, and sky feel alive. Nothing sits still.

Look at how the brushstrokes all flow in the same direction in each area. That is the secret to his movement: the direction of a brushstroke is itself a kind of line, and lines that curl and lean carry your eye along with them. This is the move you will practice in your sketch.
