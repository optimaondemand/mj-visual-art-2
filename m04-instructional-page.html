📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How can a flat drawing trick your eye into seeing a solid object?
A shape is flat. A form looks solid. The thing that turns one into the other is value, the way an artist moves from light to dark. Today you will see exactly how that works, then use it to build a stack of forms that pop off the page.

📚 PART 1 · SHAPE VERSUS FORM

Two dimensions or three?

A shape is a flat, 2D area. It has only height and width, no depth. Shapes appear when lines come together to close off a space. They can be geometric, like circles, squares, and triangles, or organic, like clouds and leaves.

A form is a 3D object. It has height, width, and depth. Forms can be geometric, like a cube, sphere, cone, cylinder, or pyramid, or organic, like rocks, people, and animals. In sculpture, a form is real and takes up actual space. In a drawing, the form is an illusion you create on a flat page.

🔍 THE SHORT VERSION
Shape = flat, height and width. Form = solid, height and width and depth. Most artists start with a shape, then build it into a form.
📚 PART 2 · HOW VALUE BUILDS A FORM

Light, midtone, and shadow

Value is how light or dark something is. It is the tool that makes a flat shape look like a real form. When you shade an object, you usually show three things: a highlight where light hits hardest, a midtone in the middle, and a shadow on the side turned away from the light. The smooth move between them is what your eye reads as a curved, solid surface.

Value also creates contrast, a strong difference between light and dark. Strong contrast makes a form pop. You will practice these forms in your sketch and final project.

📚 PART 3 · PLAYING WITH THE ILLUSION

Optical illusions and impossible forms

Once you know that value creates the illusion of depth, you can bend that illusion on purpose. An optical illusion is an image that tricks the eye into seeing something that cannot exist. An impossible form is a drawing that looks solid and 3D at first glance, but could never be built in the real world, because its parts connect in ways that do not add up.

These tricks work because of unity: every part of the drawing uses the same shading rules, so your brain accepts the whole thing as one solid object, even when it secretly does not make sense. That tension between "looks real" and "cannot be real" is what makes impossible forms so fun to look at.

💡 KEY IDEA
Strong, consistent value sells the illusion. The more each form reads as solid, the more your eye believes the impossible stack.
