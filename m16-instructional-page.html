📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How can an artist make you feel something just by looking at a picture?
Some art tries to copy exactly what the eye sees. Other art tries to show a feeling instead. That second kind is called Expressionism. Today you meet the Norwegian artist Edvard Munch and look closely at his famous painting The Scream. Then this week you will make your own version of it that shows an emotion you choose.
🎯 I WILL BE ABLE TO
  • Explain what makes Expressionist art different from art that copies exactly what the eye sees
  • Describe how Edvard Munch used color, line, and distortion to show emotion in his paintings
  • Create an original artwork that uses expressive line, color for mood, and distortion to show a feeling you choose
🎨 WHAT IS EXPRESSIONISM?

Expressionism is a style of art where the goal is to show how something feels, not exactly how it looks. An Expressionist artist might use a strange color, a wavy line, or a stretched-out shape on purpose so you can feel the mood right away.

Three tools do most of the work in this style. Keep them in mind as you look at the real paintings below.

Expressive line
Wavy, swirling lines that look like they are moving or vibrating.
Color for mood
Colors chosen for how they feel, not for how things really look.
Distortion
Stretching or bending a figure or scene to show a strong feeling.

📚 PART 1 · MEET THE ARTIST

Edvard Munch

Edvard Munch was a Norwegian artist, born in 1863 A.D. He painted about feelings such as worry, fear, love, and loneliness. Instead of trying to make his paintings look perfectly real, he showed emotions using bold colors, wavy lines, and unusual shapes.

Munch believed art should show how people feel, not just what they look like. His paintings often show people who seem worried, alone, or deep in thought. This way of working helped inspire the movement called Expressionism.

👁 PART 2 · LOOK CLOSELY

Edvard Munch, The Scream

Edvard Munch, The Scream, 1910 A.D. Public domain.

A thin figure stands on a bridge with both hands pressed to its face. Its head is bare and its mouth is open in a hollow, worried shape. Behind it, the sky is painted in streaking bands of orange, red, and yellow, while blue water swirls below and a green hillside curves on the right. Two dark figures stand far back on the bridge, calm, while the front figure looks overwhelmed.

Munch said he got the idea from a real moment. He wrote that he was walking outside at sunset when the sky turned a deep red, and he suddenly felt very anxious. The figure is not meant to be one real person. It stands for a feeling that many people have had.

🔍 FIND THE EXPRESSIONIST TOOLS
Expressive line: almost everything wiggles and swirls, even the sky and water. Color for mood: the hot red-and-orange sky makes the scene feel uneasy, not pretty. Distortion: the figure is stretched and wavy, like a melting candle, so it looks the way panic feels.
📚 PART 3 · THE SAME TOOLS, A DIFFERENT MOOD

Edvard Munch, The Storm

Edvard Munch, The Storm, 1893 A.D. Public domain.

Here Munch uses the same Expressionist tools to build a different feeling. The night is a deep blue-grey, and a single house glows with warm yellow windows. A cluster of pale figures on the left raise their hands toward their heads, while one figure in white stands apart in front.

There is no realistic detail. The shapes are soft and blurred, the colors are heavy and dark, and the lone figure feels small and uneasy. Munch chose color for mood and distortion to make a calm-looking village feel tense and a little lonely.
